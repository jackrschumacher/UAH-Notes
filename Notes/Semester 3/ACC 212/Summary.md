# Summary: Job Order Costing & Lean Management Systems
- ## Chapter M:2: Job Order Costing
- ### 1. Costing Systems Overview
  * **Purpose:** Managers need cost data to plan for and control resources, set profitable sales prices, and determine product mix.
  * **Job Order Costing:** Used by companies that manufacture unique products or provide specialized services (e.g., custom furniture, accounting firms, health-care providers).
  * **Process Costing:** Used by companies producing identical units through a series of steps (e.g., soft drink companies, medical equipment manufacturers).
  * **Job Cost Record:** A document that tracks direct materials, direct labor, and manufacturing overhead for an individual job.
- ### 2. Cost Flow & Journal Entries
  * **Accumulating Costs:**
    * **Raw Materials:** Purchasing materials increases assets (Raw Materials Inventory) and liabilities (Accounts Payable).
    * **Direct Labor:** Increases Work-in-Process (WIP) Inventory and increases Wages Payable.
    * **Indirect Costs:** Indirect materials and indirect labor are accumulated in the *Manufacturing Overhead* account.
  * **Assigning & Allocating:**
    * Direct costs are **assigned** to specific jobs.
    * Manufacturing overhead is **allocated** using a predetermined rate.
  * **Completion & Sale:**
    * **Cost of Goods Manufactured (COGM):** When a job is finished, costs move from WIP to Finished Goods Inventory.
    * **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):** When sold, costs move from Finished Goods to COGS (expense). A second entry records the Revenue earned.
- ### 3. Manufacturing Overhead (MOH)
  * **Definition:** Indirect costs not easily traced to individual jobs, such as plant utilities, rent, depreciation, and insurance.
  * **Predetermined Overhead Allocation Rate:** Calculated at the beginning of the period.
    * **Formula:** $$\text{Total Estimated Overhead Costs} \div \text{Total Estimated Allocation Base}$$
    * **Allocation Base:** A cost driver that links overhead to products (e.g., direct labor hours, machine hours).
  * **Allocation Entry:** Debit *Work-in-Process Inventory*; Credit *Manufacturing Overhead*.
- ### 4. Adjusting Overhead
  At the end of the period, the Manufacturing Overhead account is adjusted:
  * **Underallocated:** Actual overhead costs > Allocated costs.
  * **Overallocated:** Actual overhead costs < Allocated costs.
  * **Adjustment Entry:** The difference is typically adjusted to Cost of Goods Sold.

- ## Chapter M:4: Lean Management Systems & Activity-Based Costing
- ### 1. Overhead Allocation Methods
  * **Single Plantwide Rate:** The traditional method using one allocation base for the whole plant. It is simple but can be inaccurate if products use resources differently.
  * **Departmental Rates:** A refinement that uses separate rates for each department, reflecting how products use specific departmental resources.
  * **Activity-Based Costing (ABC):** Focuses on "activities" as the fundamental cost objects, allocating indirect costs based on the specific activities each product uses.
- ### 2. Activity-Based Costing (ABC) Mechanics
  * **Four Steps to Develop ABC:**
    1. Identify activities and estimate total indirect costs.
    2. Identify the allocation base for each activity and estimate the total quantity.
    3. Compute the predetermined overhead rate for each activity.
    4. Allocate indirect costs to the cost object.
  * **Hierarchy of Activities:**
    * **Unit-level:** Occurs for every individual unit (e.g., machining a part).
    * **Batch-level:** Occurs for a group of products (e.g., machine setups).
    * **Product-level:** Affects an entire product line.
    * **Facility-level:** Affects the entire production process (e.g., plant management).
- ### 3. Just-In-Time (JIT) Management
  * **Concept:** A lean system where products are produced "just in time" to meet customer demand, minimizing inventory and waste.
  * **Work Cells:** Self-contained areas with all necessary machinery and labor to complete a product, reducing movement and increasing productivity.
  * **JIT Costing (Backflush Costing):**
    * Combines Raw Materials and WIP into **Raw and In-Process Inventory**.
    * Combines Direct Labor and Overhead into **Conversion Costs**.
    * Often works backwards, assigning costs to sold units and inventories after completion.
- ### 4. Quality Management Systems (QMS)
  QMS focuses on continuous improvement to increase customer satisfaction and profits. Costs of quality are categorized as:
  * **Prevention Costs:** Incurred to avoid poor quality (e.g., R&D, training).
  * **Appraisal Costs:** Incurred to detect poor quality (e.g., testing, inspection).
  * **Internal Failure Costs:** Detected *before* delivery (e.g., rework, scrap).
  * **External Failure Costs:** Detected *after* delivery (e.g., warranty claims, lost sales); usually the most expensive.
